* Introduction
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: What Vellumory is, why it exists, and what it is not.
:END:
#+cindex: introduction
#+cindex: overview

Welcome.  Vellumory lets you read PDF documents in Emacs as comfortably as
in a dedicated viewer, and then do more with them than most viewers
allow: inspect how a document is built, check it against archival and
accessibility standards, and change it without ever losing an earlier
version.

This chapter explains what Vellumory is and what it is not.  If you would
rather start reading right away, skip to [[*Getting started]].

** What Vellumory is
#+cindex: worker program
#+cindex: architecture

Vellumory is a document reader for GNU Emacs.  It shows PDF pages in an
ordinary Emacs buffer, sharp and continuous, and keeps up while you
scroll.  When the pages are too wide for your window, or you would simply
rather read text, its reading mode reflows the document into one column
of text that fits the window.  It also reads XPS, EPUB, CBZ comic books and FB2 books, and,
through converters you may already have installed, DVI, PostScript, DjVu
and office documents.

It is made of three parts that work together:

- An Emacs Lisp package.  This is the reader you see: the pages, the
  sidebars, search, selection, the menus, Org links, evil keys and a Doom
  Emacs module.
- A worker program called ~vellumory~, written in Lean 4 with a small C
  bridge to the MuPDF library.  Emacs starts it in the background.  It
  renders pages on several threads, extracts text, reads the document's
  structure and carries out edits.
- The same program as a command line tool.  Scripts can inspect, validate,
  compare and edit documents exactly as the reader does.

The name joins /vellum/, the prepared skin that medieval books were written
on, and /memory/: Vellumory remembers where you were in every document,
and every state a document has ever been in.

** Why it exists
#+cindex: motivation

Reading inside Emacs keeps documents next to your notes, your code and
your mail.  Until now that came with compromises.  DocView converts pages
with external programs before you see them.  pdf-tools renders a page at
a time on Emacs's main thread, so large or complex pages make Emacs wait.

Vellumory was built to remove those compromises:

- Pages appear quickly and stay sharp at your screen's resolution.  Only
  the parts of pages that a window shows are rendered at full resolution,
  in parallel, away from Emacs's main thread.  While you scroll faster
  than pages can be rendered, quick previews fill in and sharpen as soon
  as you stop.
- Your work is safe.  Every change to a document is recorded.  You can
  undo it, redo it, or go back to any earlier state, byte for byte.
- Reading adapts to you.  Page colours follow a dark theme, a reading
  mode reflows the text to any window width, and resting the mouse on a
  reference shows where it leads without leaving your place.
- Documents are transparent.  A report shows fonts, images, metadata,
  attachments, signatures and claimed standards.  Validation with veraPDF
  tells you whether a document really is PDF/A or PDF/UA.
- Claims are measured.  The speed figures in this manual come with the
  method used to obtain them, so you can repeat them on your own machine
  (see [[*Performance and how it is measured]]).

** What Vellumory is not
#+cindex: limitations
#+cindex: what is missing

It is only fair to say plainly what Vellumory does not do, so that you are
not surprised later.

- It is not a page-layout editor.  You cannot change the text printed on a
  page, move images or draw.  Vellumory's edits work on the document's
  structure: pages, metadata, attachments, annotations on selected text,
  notes, form values, stamps, encryption, text recognition and, for
  experts, the raw PDF syntax.
- It needs a graphical Emacs.  Pages are images, so a text terminal cannot
  show them.
- It is not yet in a package archive such as MELPA.  You build the worker
  program yourself; Nix makes that one command (see [[*Installation]]).
- It never runs JavaScript embedded in documents.  It cannot show dynamic
  XFA forms, because MuPDF does not render them.
- It verifies digital signatures but cannot sign documents.
- It has no print command.  Print the file with your system's tools.
- It needs Emacs 29.1 or newer.  Exporting Org to accessible PDF needs
  Org 9.7 or newer (Emacs 30's, or Org from GNU ELPA).
- It is young.  This manual describes version 0.1.1, its second release.
  It has been developed and tested on Linux (x86_64), with Emacs 31.1 and
  the development version of Emacs 32.

Some properties of the worker are proved in Lean: 23 theorems about the
render scheduler, the edit history, page geometry, argument passing,
navigation and the comparison algorithm.  They are checked to use only
Lean's standard axioms.  They do not prove MuPDF, qpdf or veraPDF correct,
and they are no substitute for the tests.

** A first session
#+cindex: tour
#+cindex: first session

Here is what a first session can look like.  The keys are explained in
the chapters that follow.

1. Visit a PDF with ~C-x C-f~.  The first page appears, with page previews
   in a sidebar on the left.
2. Press ~SPC~ to read on and ~DEL~ to go back.  ~n~ and ~p~ turn whole
   pages.
3. Type ~/ trace RET~.  Every match of "trace" is highlighted on the
   pages; ~M-n~ moves to the next one.
4. Drag over a sentence with the mouse and press ~M-w~.  The text is in
   the kill ring, ready to paste into your notes.
5. With the sentence still selected, press ~a h~.  The sentence is now
   highlighted in the file itself, as an annotation.
6. Changed your mind?  ~C-/~ undoes the edit and puts the file back
   exactly as it was.
7. Press ~C-c C-r~ to read the text reflowed to your window, in a size and
   colours that suit you; ~q~ takes you back to the pages, at the place you
   reached.
8. Press ~?~ to see every command in one menu.
9. Press ~q~ to put the document away.  The next time you open it, it
   opens where you left off.

** About this manual
#+cindex: conventions

Keys are written as Emacs writes them: ~C-x~ means hold Control and press
x, ~M-x~ means Meta (usually Alt) and x.  ~RET~ is the Return key, ~SPC~ the
space bar and ~DEL~ the Backspace key.  Every command can also be run by
name with ~M-x~.

The keys in the main chapters are those of plain Emacs.  If you use evil
or Doom Emacs, see [[*Evil and Doom Emacs]] for their keys; the commands are
the same.

Where a feature has limits, the section says so.  If you find that the
manual and the program disagree, the program is right and the manual has
a bug: please report it.

* Installation
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: Requirements, the worker program, and every way to install.
:END:
#+cindex: installation

Vellumory has two parts to install: the Emacs Lisp package and the worker
program it talks to.  The Nix flake builds both in one step, and it is the
tested way.  The other recipes in this chapter install the Lisp from the
source repository; you then build the worker separately.

The source repository is https://github.com/cypl0x/vellumory.

** Requirements
#+cindex: requirements
#+cindex: Emacs version
#+cindex: graphical display

You need:

- GNU Emacs 29.1 or newer, running on a graphical display (X, Wayland or
  the pgtk build) with image support.  In a text terminal the reader
  refuses to start with the message "Vellumory needs a graphical Emacs
  with image support".  Vellumory was developed on Emacs 31.1 and the
  development version, Emacs 32, where its graphical tests run too.  Its
  Emacs test suite also passes in batch mode on Emacs 29.4 and 30.2, and
  the project's continuous integration is set up to run it on 29.4, 30.2,
  31.1 and the development snapshot.
- For exporting Org to accessible, archival PDF: Org 9.7 or newer, which
  is Emacs 30's; on Emacs 29 install Org from GNU ELPA.
- Linux.  The Nix flake builds for =x86_64-linux= and =aarch64-linux=.  All
  tests and measurements in this manual were made on x86_64 Linux.  Other
  systems have not been tried.
- The worker program ~vellumory~ (see [[*The worker program]]).

Some features use other programs.  Each one is optional; without it, only
the features in its row are unavailable.

| Program                                    | What it enables                                          |
|--------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------|
| qpdf (with its fix-qdf)                    | every edit, extracting, splitting, merging, syntax view  |
| veraPDF                                    | validation, batch validation, checks after Org export    |
| ocrmypdf with Tesseract                    | text recognition (OCR)                                   |
| LuaLaTeX from TeX Live 2025 or newer       | Org export to accessible PDF through LaTeX               |
| Typst 0.14 or newer and the ox-typst package | Org export through Typst                               |
| dvipdfmx                                   | reading DVI files                                        |
| ps2pdf (Ghostscript)                       | reading PostScript                                       |
| ddjvu (DjVuLibre)                          | reading DjVu                                             |
| soffice (LibreOffice)                      | reading office documents                                 |

In the Nix build, the worker has the exact paths of qpdf, fix-qdf, veraPDF
and ocrmypdf compiled in, and ocrmypdf brings its own Tesseract.  Built any
other way, it finds them on your =PATH=.  ~M-x vellumory-doctor~ tells you
which ones it finds (see [[*Checking that it works]]).

Several Emacs packages add to Vellumory when you have them, and none is
required: evil, Doom Emacs, AUCTeX, org-noter, consult, embark and
ox-typst.  The transient library, which draws the command menu, comes with
Emacs.

** The worker program
#+cindex: worker program
#+vindex: vellumory-program

The worker is the program that does the heavy lifting.  The reader starts
it with ~vellumory serve~ when you open your first document.  One worker
serves every document of your Emacs session, sharing its render threads by
priority, and it exits when you close the last document.

The option ~vellumory-program~ names it.  By default Vellumory looks for the
worker next to its own Lisp files, in two places:

- In a Nix build, the Lisp lives in =share/emacs/site-lisp/vellumory= and the
  worker in =bin/vellumory= of the same package.
- In a source checkout, the Lisp lives in =lisp/= and the worker is built in
  =.lake/build/bin/vellumory=.

If neither exists, Vellumory runs whatever ~vellumory~ your =exec-path=
finds.  If you keep the worker elsewhere, set the option:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq vellumory-program "/path/to/bin/vellumory")
#+end_src

*** Building the worker with Nix
#+cindex: Nix, building the worker

In a checkout of the repository:

#+begin_example
git clone https://github.com/cypl0x/vellumory
cd vellumory
nix build path:.
#+end_example

This builds the worker and the Lisp, and runs the native test suite and
the Emacs tests as part of the build, so the first build takes a while.
The result is a link =result= with the worker =result/bin/vellumory=, and in
=result/share/emacs/site-lisp/vellumory= the Lisp, its generated autoloads
and this manual.

*** Building the worker in the development shell
#+cindex: development shell

For working on Vellumory itself, the flake has a development shell with
every tool the tests need:

#+begin_example
nix develop path:.
lean --run scripts/Build.lean prepare
lean --run scripts/Build.lean lake build vellumory
#+end_example

The worker is then =.lake/build/bin/vellumory=, which the Lisp in =lisp/=
of the same checkout finds by itself.  A checkout has no autoloads file;
make one with =lean --run scripts/Build.lean autoloads=, which writes
=lisp/vellumory-autoloads.el=.

*** Building without Nix
#+cindex: building without Nix

Building without Nix is possible in principle but not documented or
tested yet.  You would need Lean 4.30.0 with Lake, the lean4-cli library
(=lean --run scripts/Build.lean prepare PATH= copies it into the checkout),
MuPDF 1.27.2 (the worker links =libmupdf.so.27.2=), OpenSSL, TeX Live's
libsynctex and Exempi.  =lakefile.lean= takes their locations as =-K=
options (=mupdfInclude=, =mupdfLib=, =opensslInclude= and so on), and
=scripts/Build.lean= shows how the pieces fit together.  If you try it,
your notes would be very welcome.

Prebuilt worker programs are planned but not available yet.

** Nix flake
#+cindex: Nix flake

The flake offers these outputs, for =x86_64-linux= and =aarch64-linux=:

- ~packages.SYSTEM.default~ (also called ~vellumory~) :: the worker in
  =bin/=, and the Lisp, its generated autoloads and this manual in
  =share/emacs/site-lisp/vellumory=.
- ~packages.SYSTEM.lisp~ :: the Emacs Lisp package alone, byte-compiled by
  Emacs's own package builder, without the worker.
- ~devShells.SYSTEM.default~ :: every tool the build, the tests and the
  benchmarks use.
- ~checks.SYSTEM.tooling~ :: the full build with its tests.

The other packages (the test documents and the Doom review build) serve
the project's own tests and benchmarks.  There is no overlay and no NixOS
or home-manager module yet.

The simplest way to use a build is to add its Lisp directory to your
=load-path=:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(add-to-list 'load-path "/path/to/result/share/emacs/site-lisp/vellumory")
(require 'vellumory-autoloads)
(vellumory-install)
#+end_src

Because the worker sits beside that Lisp directory, Vellumory finds it
without further settings, and ~vellumory-info~ finds the manual there too.

In a home-manager configuration, the same three lines with the package's
store path do the job.  This sketch has not been tested:

#+begin_example
# flake.nix: inputs.vellumory.url = "github:cypl0x/vellumory";
{ inputs, pkgs, ... }:
let
  vellumory = inputs.vellumory.packages.${pkgs.stdenv.hostPlatform.system}.default;
in {
  home.packages = [ vellumory ];   # the vellumory command line tool
  programs.emacs = {
    enable = true;
    extraConfig = ''
      (add-to-list 'load-path "${vellumory}/share/emacs/site-lisp/vellumory")
      (require 'vellumory-autoloads)
      (vellumory-install)
    '';
  };
}
#+end_example

If you use the ~lisp~ package instead, as an ordinary Emacs package, it has
no worker beside it: put the worker on your =PATH= or set
~vellumory-program~.

#+cindex: OCR languages
#+cindex: Tesseract languages
Text recognition comes with every language Tesseract knows, which takes
about a gigabyte.  To keep only the languages you read, override the
package:

#+begin_example
vellumory.override { tesseractLanguages = [ "eng" "deu" ]; }
#+end_example

Tesseract's orientation and script detection (=osd=), which ocrmypdf uses,
is always kept.

** Doom Emacs
#+cindex: Doom Emacs, installing

The repository contains a Doom module, =:tools vellumory=, in
=doom/tools/vellumory=.  It replaces Doom's =:tools pdf=; enable one of the
two, not both.

1. Clone the repository and build the worker (see [[*The worker
   program]]), for example in =~/src/vellumory=.
2. Link the module into your private modules:
   #+begin_example
   mkdir -p "${DOOMDIR:-$HOME/.config/doom}/modules/tools"
   ln -s ~/src/vellumory/doom/tools/vellumory \
         "${DOOMDIR:-$HOME/.config/doom}/modules/tools/vellumory"
   #+end_example
3. In =init.el=, add =vellumory= under =:tools= (and remove =pdf=).  The
   module has no flags.
4. Tell Vellumory where the worker is, in =config.el=:
   #+begin_src emacs-lisp
   (setq vellumory-program (expand-file-name "~/src/vellumory/result/bin/vellumory"))
   #+end_src
5. Run =doom sync=, restart Emacs, and run =doom doctor=.

The module's =packages.el= installs the Lisp with Doom's usual package
manager (straight.el) from GitHub; if the environment variable
=VELLUMORY_SOURCE= names a checkout, it builds from that checkout instead.
With nix-doom-emacs-unstraightened, the module leaves the package to Nix:
add the flake's ~lisp~ output to =extraPackages= and its ~default~ output to
=extraBinPackages=, which puts the worker on Emacs's =PATH=.

=doom doctor= runs the same checks as ~M-x vellumory-doctor~ (see [[*Checking
that it works]]), reporting errors as errors and warnings as warnings.  It
also warns if =:tools pdf= is enabled too, and if TeX Live lacks the LaTeX
tagging code that Org export needs.

What the module sets up is described in [[*The Doom module]].  The route
through nix-doom-emacs-unstraightened is the one the project tests; the
straight.el route from GitHub has not been tested yet.

** package-vc and use-package
#+cindex: package-vc
#+cindex: use-package

Emacs can install packages straight from their repository with
package-vc, and since Emacs 30 use-package can too.  Vellumory's Lisp lives
in the =lisp= subdirectory, and its manual in =docs=:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(package-vc-install
 '(vellumory :url "https://github.com/cypl0x/vellumory"
             :lisp-dir "lisp"
             :doc "docs/vellumory.texi"))
(vellumory-install)
#+end_src

With use-package's =:vc= keyword:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(use-package vellumory
  :vc (:url "https://github.com/cypl0x/vellumory"
       :lisp-dir "lisp" :doc "docs/vellumory.texi" :rev :newest)
  :defer t
  :init (vellumory-install))
#+end_src

The =:doc= entry builds this manual and installs it into Info, so
~vellumory-info~ finds it, and package-vc generates the autoloads.
package-vc keeps the whole repository, so if you build the worker inside
that checkout (in the development shell, see [[*Building the worker in the
development shell]]), Vellumory finds it there without further settings;
otherwise set ~vellumory-program~.  These recipes follow the documented
package-vc format but have not been tested.

** Setting up your init file
#+cindex: init file
#+cindex: file associations

Once the package is on your =load-path=, two lines are enough:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(require 'vellumory-autoloads)   ; skip this if your package manager loads it
(vellumory-install)
#+end_src

The autoloads file is generated, not part of the repository.  Package
managers make their own, and the Nix package contains one.  In a plain
clone, make it once in the clone's top directory, with
=lean --run scripts/Build.lean autoloads= in the development shell, or
with Emacs alone:

#+begin_example
emacs --batch -Q --eval '(loaddefs-generate "lisp" (expand-file-name "lisp/vellumory-autoloads.el") (list "vellumory-autoloads.el") nil nil t)'
#+end_example

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: M-x vellumory-install :: Make Emacs open documents in Vellumory.  It
  associates the extensions in ~vellumory-file-extensions~ and every file
  that starts with =%PDF= with ~vellumory-mode~.  It also associates the
  converted formats whose converter is installed (except those in
  ~vellumory-converted-not-visited~), installs a file handler that visits
  documents without reading their bytes into the buffer, skips Emacs's
  large-file question for documents, prepares Org links, the AUCTeX viewer
  and org-noter for when those load, and binds ~;~ in Dired (see
  ~vellumory-dired-key~).  It does not load the reader itself; that
  happens when you open the first document.
- Key: M-x vellumory-uninstall :: Undo all of that.

You do not have to install anything to try Vellumory: ~M-x vellumory-open~
reads any file in Vellumory, associations or not.

If you also use pdf-tools or DocView, the reader installed last wins,
because each adds its association to the front of Emacs's lists.  Call
~vellumory-install~ after ~pdf-tools-install~ if you want Vellumory to open
PDFs.

For evil, add:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(with-eval-after-load 'evil
  (require 'vellumory-evil)
  (vellumory-evil-setup))
#+end_src

** Reading this manual in Emacs
#+cindex: Info manual

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: M-x vellumory-info :: Open this manual in Info.  It is also on ~M~ in
  the command menu (~?~) of a document, and on ~m~ in that menu elsewhere.

~vellumory-info~ looks for =vellumory.info= beside Vellumory's Lisp files
first, where the Nix package installs it, and then in the directories
Info searches, where the package-vc recipes above install it.  The ~lisp~
package of the flake and the Doom module's straight.el recipe do not
install it.

To build it from a checkout, run this in the development shell (it needs
=makeinfo= from GNU Texinfo):

#+begin_example

#+end_example

That writes =target/vellumory.info=.  Then tell Info where it is:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(add-to-list 'Info-additional-directory-list "/path/to/vellumory/target/")
#+end_src

You can also build the manual anywhere you like with
=makeinfo --no-split -o vellumory.info docs/vellumory.texi=.

** Checking that it works
#+cindex: checking the installation
#+cindex: doctor
#+findex: vellumory-doctor-mode

Vellumory can check its own setup and tell you what to fix.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: M-x vellumory-doctor :: Check everything Vellumory relies on and
  show the results in a report.  It is also ~!~ in the command menu.

The report has six sections:

- Emacs :: the version, a graphical frame, image support, PNG support, the
  display, and how garbage collection is handled;
- Worker :: whether the worker program is found, answers, speaks the
  protocol this version of the Lisp expects, and renders with MuPDF;
- Opening PDF files :: whether PDF files really open in Vellumory, and
  whether another reader would take them over;
- Programs for particular features :: qpdf, veraPDF, Tesseract, ocrmypdf,
  LuaLaTeX, Typst and the converters, each with what it enables and how to
  get it.  The worker tells the doctor which programs it runs itself: a
  Nix-built worker's own qpdf, veraPDF and ocrmypdf (with the Tesseract
  that comes with it) count as found even when they are not on your
  =PATH=;
- Integration :: evil, Doom Emacs, the icon font, and this manual;
- Directories :: the directories Vellumory writes to.

Each check is marked as fine, informational, a warning or an error, with
a short explanation.  Where Emacs can fix a problem itself, such as making
Vellumory the reader for PDF files or setting up evil's keys, the report
offers a button.

In the report:

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: g (vellumory-doctor) :: Run the checks again.
- Key: w (vellumory-doctor-copy) :: Copy the report as plain text, for a bug
  report.

* Getting started
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: Opening documents, the screen, the menu and the mouse.
:END:
#+cindex: getting started

This chapter covers what you need for everyday reading: opening a
document, finding your way around the screen, the menu that holds every

** Opening a document
#+cindex: opening documents
#+findex: vellumory-mode

After ~vellumory-install~, documents open in Vellumory wherever Emacs visits
files: with ~C-x C-f~, from Dired, from bookmarks, from Org links.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: M-x vellumory-open :: Read a file in Vellumory, whatever its name.
  This works without ~vellumory-install~.
- Key: M-x vellumory-project-documents :: Choose one of the current
  project's PDFs and open it.

When a document opens, the buffer holds no copy of the file.  The worker
reads the file itself, so even a document of hundreds of megabytes opens
without Emacs reading it into memory.  The first pages appear, and the
page previews open in a sidebar on the left (see
~vellumory-show-thumbnails~).

Documents can say how they want to be shown.  If a PDF asks for two
columns, it opens with two pages side by side; if it asks for its
contents, the contents sidebar opens instead of the previews.  The option
~vellumory-follow-document-display~ turns this off.

#+cindex: encrypted documents
#+cindex: password
An encrypted document asks for its password.  If the password is wrong, it
asks again.  The password stays in memory only, so that the document can
be reloaded; it is never written anywhere.

#+cindex: remote files
#+cindex: TRAMP
A document on a remote machine (through TRAMP), or one in a buffer without
a file, is copied into Vellumory's cache under a name made from its
content, and read from there.  You can read and search it, but editing
needs a local file.

** What you see
#+cindex: header line
#+cindex: mode line

The header line above the pages shows where you are:

#+begin_example
 Page 3/14   100%   Ready
#+end_example

That is the page in view, the number of pages, the zoom and the status.
While pages render, the status reads "Rendering…".  If the document has
page labels that differ from the page numbers, the label comes first and
the number in parentheses, as in =iv (4)=.  With two pages side by side,
both are named, as in =2–3/14=.  During a search the search status is
added, and the keyboard caret shows when it is active.  The mode line
shows the page as well.  To hide the header line, set
~vellumory-header-line~ to ~nil~ (the Doom module does, because its mode line
shows the same).

Between the pages you see the "desk", a neutral background (the face
~vellumory-desk~).  The sidebar on the left shows either page previews or
the document's contents; the tabs at its top switch between them.

** Leaving and coming back
#+cindex: reading position
#+cindex: saving place
#+cindex: save-place-mode
#+vindex: vellumory-state-directory

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: q :: Put the document away (~quit-window~).

Vellumory remembers exactly where you were: the page, the position on the
page and the zoom.  It saves the place whenever Emacs has been idle for
two seconds, and when you close the document.  The place is stored twice, once
for the file's name and once for its content, so a renamed or copied
document opens at the same place too.  The files are small JSON files in
~vellumory-state-directory~ (by default =~/.local/state/vellumory=).

Emacs's own ~save-place-mode~ is not used for documents: their buffer
position means nothing, and Vellumory keeps a more exact place itself.  It
drops a document's old save-place entry the next time the document is
closed.

#+cindex: reloading
#+cindex: watching files

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: r (vellumory-reload) ::
- Key: R :: Read the file again, keeping your place.  ~M-x revert-buffer~
  does the same.

You rarely need to reload by hand.  When the file changes on disk, for
instance because LaTeX has compiled it again, Vellumory reloads it by
itself once the file has been quiet for a moment (~vellumory-watch-file~).
This also works when a program replaces the file by renaming a new one
over it.

#+cindex: saving
Saving with ~C-x C-s~ never changes the document.  The buffer holds only the
page layout, and Vellumory never writes that into your file.  Files change
only through the edits described in [[*Editing safely]].  ~C-x C-w~ writes a
copy of the document instead (see [[*Documents that cannot be edited]]).

** Page colours and dark themes
#+cindex: dark theme

Pages can be shown in four colourings:

- Key: M-x vellumory-theme-tint ::
- Key: M-x vellumory-theme-invert ::
- Key: M-x vellumory-theme-auto :: Choose one of the four directly, for
  binding to keys of your own.
- Key: M-x vellumory-night-mode :: Turn night mode on or off for this

(~vellumory-dispatch-colours~): ~c~ cycles, ~o~, ~t~, ~i~ and ~a~ choose original,

drawn again to match.  Set ~vellumory-theme~ to start every document in the

** Reading mode
#+cindex: reading mode
#+cindex: reflow
#+cindex: narrow windows
#+findex: vellumory-reflow-mode
#+findex: vellumory-reflow-mode-menu
#+findex: vellumory-dispatch-reflow
#+findex: vellumory-reflow-customize
#+findex: vellumory-reflow-set-colours

A page is made for paper.  In a narrow window, or when you simply want to
read, reading mode shows the document as text that flows to the window:
one column at a comfortable line length, in the size, font and colours
you choose, much as the reading modes of PDF apps on phones do.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: C-c C-r (vellumory-reflow) :: Read this document reflowed to the
  window; in reading mode, return to the pages.  It is ~m~ in the command
  menu and "Reading mode (reflowed)" in the right-click menu.

Reading mode is a view.  The document is not changed, and leaving it
with ~q~ shows the pages in the same window, at the place you reached.  The
place you reach is also the one Vellumory remembers for the document.  The
reflowed text lives in a buffer of its own, =*Reflow: NAME*=, whose header
line names the page you are on.

*** Where the text comes from

The worker builds each page's text anew.  When the PDF is tagged, it
follows the document's structure tree: paragraphs, headings, lists,
tables, code, notes and figures keep their kind.  When it is not, it reads
the page's layout: each block of text becomes a paragraph, much larger
type a heading, and monospaced blocks code.  The lines of a paragraph flow
together, with hyphens at line ends joined; code keeps its line breaks and
indentation.

Figures, formulas, images and drawings are shown as pictures cut from the
page, sized so that their lettering is as large as the text around them,
never wider than the text and never taller than most of the window.
Their text becomes the pictures' alternative text, which ~I~ shows
instead.  Running heads, page numbers and other text outside the content
are left out: in a tagged document, the parts its structure marks as not
belonging to the content; in an untagged one, single lines in the
margins.  ~H~ shows them.  The page number where each page begins is shown
small (~B~ hides it).

Links work: one within the document goes to its page, here in reading
mode; one to the web opens with ~browse-url~.  Pages are loaded as you
read, in either direction, and about fourteen pages around the one you
read are kept in the buffer.

*** Colours, size and line length

The colours come from ~vellumory-reflow-colours~:

- ~auto~ :: dark while the document's pages are inverted (night mode, or
  inverted with a dark theme), your Emacs theme's colours otherwise;
- ~theme~ :: always your Emacs theme's colours;
- ~paper~, ~sepia~, ~night~ :: the faces ~vellumory-reflow-paper~,
  ~vellumory-reflow-sepia~ and ~vellumory-reflow-night~.

In dark colours the pictures are inverted to match.  Lines hold at most
~vellumory-reflow-measure~ characters (70 by default; lines of 60 to 75
characters read best), and fewer in a narrow window; the text is centred
with margins on both sides.  The text is set in the proportional
~variable-pitch~ font (~vellumory-reflow-variable-pitch~), with a little
extra space between lines (~vellumory-reflow-line-spacing~).  All of these
are in the customization group ~vellumory-reflow~ (see [[*Reading mode
options]]).

*** Narrow windows

#+vindex: vellumory-reflow-narrow-width
Set ~vellumory-reflow-narrow-width~ to a number of columns, and a window
narrower than that shows its document in reading mode by itself, for
example when you split the frame side by side.  Once the window is wide
enough again (eight columns more), it shows the pages.  The default, ~nil~,
never does this.

*** What reading mode cannot do

How well a page reflows depends on the document.  A tagged PDF carries
its own structure and reading order.  An untagged one reflows only as
well as the reading order MuPDF finds in its layout: columns, tables and
captions may come out in an odd order, and a heading is only recognized
by its size.  Formulas and drawings are pictures, not text, so they
cannot be searched or copied there.  Nothing can be edited from reading
mode: annotating, filling in forms and the other edits work on the
pages.

advances by itself after that time (~vellumory-present-follow-durations~).
The mouse wheel turns at most one page per 0.3 seconds, so a trackpad's
stream of events does not race through the slides.

#+cindex: transitions
Page transitions that a document defines (/Trans) are drawn as it asks
(~vellumory-present-transitions~).  Wipe, Push, Cover, Uncover, Split, Box and
Blinds move the two page images, with their directions and durations.
Dissolve, Glitter, Fade and Fly are composed by the worker from both pages,
frame by frame.  On the test machine that reached about 60 frames a second
at 1278 × 958 pixels.  Other styles change the page at once, and a key
pressed during a transition finishes it.  On an Emacs with canvas images
(Emacs 32 since August 2026), composed frames replace one image's pixels
in place, which costs Emacs less time per frame (~vellumory-present-canvas~).

** Reading across windows
#+cindex: follow mode
#+cindex: columns

On a wide screen you can read a document in columns, as ~follow-mode~ does
for text: each window starts exactly where the one before it ends.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: M-x vellumory-follow-split :: Show the document in side-by-side
  windows that follow each other, two by default; a numeric prefix sets the
  number of columns.  It is on ~F~ in the command menu.
- Key: M-x vellumory-follow-mode :: Turn the following on or off for the
  windows that already show the document in this frame.

The windows are chained in follow-mode's order: top to bottom, then left
to right.  Scrolling any of them moves all of them, and a screen is all of
them together.  Unlike ~follow-mode~, which chains windows by buffer
positions, Vellumory chains them by pixels, so a page can continue from
the foot of one column to the head of the next.  Each window's header line
names its own page.  If you turn on ~follow-mode~ in a document, Vellumory
turns on its own mode instead.

** Other formats
#+cindex: formats
#+cindex: EPUB
#+cindex: XPS
#+cindex: CBZ
#+cindex: FB2

MuPDF reads several formats besides PDF, and so does Vellumory: XPS and
OpenXPS, CBZ comic book archives, and the e-book formats EPUB and FB2.
~vellumory-file-extensions~ lists the extensions that ~vellumory-install~
associates.

EPUB and FB2 books have no fixed pages.  Vellumory lays them out on pages
of ~vellumory-reflow-page-size~ points (450 × 600 by default), with text of
~vellumory-reflow-text-size~ points (11 by default), and applies the CSS file
~vellumory-reflow-stylesheet~ if you set one.  The text-scale keys change the
size for the book in view (see [[*Zoom and fit]]).  Each layout is a document
of its own as far as rendering and saved places are concerned, so nothing
from one size leaks into another.

*** Converted formats
#+cindex: DVI
#+cindex: PostScript
#+cindex: DjVu
#+cindex: office documents
#+cindex: conversion

Like DocView, Vellumory reads some formats by converting them to PDF:

| Format                           | Converter                   |
|----------------------------------+-----------------------------|
| DVI                              | dvipdfmx                    |
| PostScript and EPS               | ps2pdf (Ghostscript)        |
| DjVu                             | ddjvu (DjVuLibre)           |
| OpenDocument, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, RTF | soffice (LibreOffice) |

~vellumory-converters~ holds these rules, and you can change them.  A format
is associated only when its converter is installed.  PostScript is left
to ~ps-mode~, where it is usually edited: read a PostScript file with ~M-x
vellumory-converted-mode~ (~vellumory-converted-not-visited~ lists the formats
treated this way).

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: M-x vellumory-converted-mode :: Read the file in this buffer
  converted to PDF.

The conversion is cached under ~vellumory-cache-directory~, keyed by the
original's content and the converter, so each version converts only once.
When the original changes, for example a DVI that TeX has written again,
it is converted again and shown at the same place.  While a conversion
runs, the buffer says so.  Converted documents can be read, searched and
selected like any PDF, but not edited: an edit would not reach the
original.  Leaving the mode shows the original file again.  The
LibreOffice route is not covered by the project's tests.

** Virtual documents
#+cindex: virtual documents
#+cindex: vpdf files
#+findex: vellumory-virtual-mode
#+findex: vellumory-virtual-view-mode
#+findex: vellumory-virtual-edit-mode

A virtual document lists pages, or parts of pages, of other documents, in
the order you want to read them.  Vellumory reads the format of pdf-tools'
=.vpdf= files, so files written for pdf-tools open unchanged:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
;; %VPDF 1.0
(("paper.pdf" (1 . 4) 7)                ; pages 1–4 and 7
 ("slides.pdf")                         ; every page
 ("scan.pdf" (2 0 0 1 0.5)))            ; the top half of page 2
#+end_src

A page specification is a page number, a range ~(FIRST . LAST)~ (where a
~LAST~ of 0 means the last page), or either followed by a region ~LEFT TOP
RIGHT BOTTOM~ in fractions of the page as shown; ~(nil . REGION)~ means every
page.  A file that begins with the line =;; %VPDF 1.0= opens as a virtual
document.

Vellumory composes the pages into one real PDF with MuPDF, cached by the
list and by every source's content, and reads that.  So everything works
on it: search, occur, selection, links, saved places.  The header line
names each page's source.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: M-x vellumory-virtual-create :: Start a new list: in Dired, of the
  marked files (~; V~); elsewhere, of every PDF in the directory.  The list
  opens for editing; ~C-c C-c~ reads it.  Save it as a =.vpdf= file to keep
  it.
- Key: C-c C-c (vellumory-virtual-edit) :: In a virtual document, edit its
  list; ~C-c C-c~ (~vellumory-virtual-mode~) shows it again.
- Key: M-} (vellumory-virtual-next-document) ::
- Key: M-{ (vellumory-virtual-previous-document) :: Go to the first page of
  the next or previous source.

A virtual document cannot be edited as a whole; edit its sources instead.

** How pages are rendered
#+cindex: rendering
#+cindex: previews while scrolling

You never need to think about rendering, but knowing a little explains
what you see.  Each page is laid out as strips of images.  Only the strips
that a window shows are rendered at full resolution, at your screen's
pixel density, on several threads (by default the processor's cores
less two, at least two and at most six).  Work for strips that scroll out of
view is cancelled.  While you move faster than pages can be rendered, a
quick low-resolution preview of the whole page fills in, and it sharpens
about a tenth of a second after you stop (~vellumory-refine-delay~).  Pages
that are cheap to draw are rendered sharp at once.  When Emacs is idle, a
screen ahead and half a screen behind are prepared.

Rendered strips are kept in a private directory under =XDG_RUNTIME_DIR=
(normally in memory) and removed when the worker exits.  Page previews and
sidebar thumbnails are also kept across sessions in
=~/.cache/vellumory/rasters=, so a document you open again shows its
previews at once.

** Searching text
#+cindex: search

** Link previews
#+cindex: link preview
#+cindex: hover preview
#+vindex: vellumory-link-preview
#+vindex: vellumory-link-preview-delay
#+vindex: vellumory-link-preview-size
#+findex: vellumory-link-preview-hide
#+findex: vellumory-link-preview-scroll-up

A reference such as "see Figure 3" or "[12]" sends you elsewhere in the
document.  Rest the mouse on such a link, and after a moment
(~vellumory-link-preview-delay~, 0.3 seconds, as in GNOME's Papers) a small
frame opens beside it and shows where the link leads, without leaving
your place.

The preview shows a strip of the target page at the zoom and in the page
colours you read in, lined up over the page's own columns, with the
target about a third of the way down.  Its header names the page.  It
opens above the link when there is room, otherwise below it.  Its size is
~vellumory-link-preview-size~: a fraction of the window's width and height,
92% by 35% by default.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: wheel (vellumory-link-preview-scroll-down) :: In the preview, the
  wheel scrolls it (~vellumory-link-preview-scroll-up~ scrolls back), so you
  can read on past its first lines.
- Key: mouse-1 (vellumory-link-preview-visit) :: In the preview, go to the
  link's target.  ~B~ (~vellumory-history-back~) returns to where you were.

Moving the mouse away from both the link and the preview closes it, and
so does any key.  Only links within the document are previewed; links to
web pages are not.  Previews need a graphical display, since they are
child frames.  ~vellumory-link-preview~ set to ~nil~ turns them off, and ~;l~ in
the command menu toggles them for the session.

** Going back
#+cindex: history of positions
#+cindex: back and forward

After following a link you will often want to return.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: B (vellumory-history-back) :: Return to where you were before the
  last link or jump.
- Key: N (vellumory-history-forward) :: Undo that.

These are the keys pdf-tools uses too.  They are also ~[~ and ~]~ in the

** Actions with embark
#+cindex: embark

With the embark package, ~embark-act~ in a document acts on the selected

* Annotations and forms
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: Marking text, notes, and filling in PDF forms.
:END:
#+cindex: annotations

Annotations are notes and markings stored in the PDF itself, so every PDF
reader shows them.  In Vellumory each annotation is an edit like any
other: it is recorded in the document's history and can be undone (see
[[*Editing safely]]).

** Marking selected text
#+cindex: highlight
#+cindex: underline
#+cindex: strike out

Select some text first (see [[*Selecting and copying text]]), then:

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t

- Key: a u (vellumory-underline) :: Underline it.
- Key: a s (vellumory-strike-out) :: Strike it out.
- Key: a q (vellumory-squiggly) :: Underline it with a squiggle.

The colours come from ~vellumory-annotation-colors~ and the author's name
from ~vellumory-annotation-author~ (your ~user-full-name~).  Each annotation is
dated with the time the edit is recorded, so replaying the same edit gives
the same bytes.

Hovering over an annotation on the page shows its text.  On the command
line, ~vellumory annotations FILE~ prints them as JSON (see [[*Inspecting from
the command line]]).

** What annotations cannot do yet
#+cindex: annotations, limits

The kinds are highlight, underline, strike-out, squiggly underline and
text notes.  There is no free-hand drawing, there are no shapes, and
annotations cannot be moved or resized; editing changes their text only.

Annotations respect the archival standard PDF/A: they are marked as
printable, as PDF/A requires.  Highlights need transparency, which PDF/A-1
forbids, so in a PDF/A-1 document Vellumory refuses to highlight and
suggests an underline instead.

** Filling in forms
#+cindex: forms
#+cindex: form fields
#+cindex: AcroForm
#+findex: vellumory-form-mode

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: e f (vellumory-fill-form) :: Show the document's form fields in a
  form buffer, =*Form: NAME*=.

Text fields take any text, checkboxes yes or no, and choice fields one of
the values shown.  Read-only fields are shown but cannot be changed, and
signature fields are left out.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: C-c C-c (vellumory-form-commit) :: Record all changed fields as one
  edit, which undo reverses.  The fields on the page are redrawn with their
  new values.
- Key: C-c C-k (vellumory-form-cancel) :: Discard the form.

You can also fill a field right on the page: click it.  A text or choice
field asks for its value, and a checkbox toggles.  Each such click is an
edit of its own.

#+cindex: JavaScript
Filling a form never runs code from the document: values are set with the
document's JavaScript triggers ignored.  Dynamic XFA forms are not
supported, because MuPDF cannot render them; the document details say when
a document has one.  On the command line, ~vellumory form FILE~ lists the
fields, and the ~fill-form~ operation fills them (see [[*Edit operations]]).

* Inspecting documents
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: Details, signatures, validation, preflight, PDF syntax.
:END:
#+cindex: inspection

A PDF can be many things inside: tagged or untagged, with embedded fonts or
without, signed, encrypted, archival.  Vellumory shows you which.

** Document details
#+cindex: document details
#+cindex: fonts
#+cindex: metadata, viewing
#+cindex: attachments, viewing
#+findex: vellumory-details-mode

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: I (vellumory-details) :: Show what the document is made of, in a
  buffer =*Details: NAME*=.

The report has these sections:

- Document :: format and version, number of pages, size, objects and
  revisions; encryption and the permissions it denies; the standards the
  document claims (PDF/A, PDF/UA and others, read from its XMP metadata);
  whether it is tagged and has a structure tree; its language; whether it
  is linearized; and whether MuPDF had to repair its structure.
- Signatures :: see [[*Signatures]].
- Metadata :: title, author, subject, keywords, creator and producer;
  creation and modification dates in readable form; whether there is XMP
  metadata.
- Content :: fonts (how many are embedded, how many have Unicode maps),
  images, annotations (links, form widgets, markup), forms (including XFA),
  JavaScript (never executed here), transparency, output intents, and
  navigation: the outline, page labels and how the document asks to open.
- Attachments :: every embedded file.  ~RET~ on one opens it; it is copied
  to Vellumory's cache first.
- Page :: for the page in view, its boxes (media, crop, bleed, trim, art)
  in points and millimetres, its rotation and user unit, and the fonts,
  images and annotations it uses.

Problems, such as fonts that are not embedded, are highlighted.  Claims
are reported as claims; whether the document really meets a standard is
what validation tells you.

In the report:

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: g (vellumory-details-refresh) :: Read the report again.
- Key: e (vellumory-details-edit-metadata) :: Edit the metadata (see
  [[*Document edits]]).
- Key: a (vellumory-details-attach) :: Attach a file.
- Key: D (vellumory-details-detach) :: Remove an attachment.
- Key: TAB :: Move to the next button, such as an attachment; ~<backtab>~
  moves back.

The command line prints the same report as JSON with ~vellumory info FILE~.

** Signatures
#+cindex: digital signatures
#+cindex: signatures, verifying

The details report verifies every digital signature itself, with OpenSSL.
For each one it tells you:

- whether the signed bytes are intact;
- who signed (the name in the certificate);
- whether the signer's certificate chains to the certificates your system
  trusts, and if not, why not (a self-signed certificate, for example);
- whether the signature covers the whole file, or the document was changed
  after signing.

Because every edit rewrites the file, an edit invalidates signatures.
Before editing a signed document, Vellumory asks.  The signed version stays
in the history, and undo restores it byte for byte, signatures intact.

Vellumory verifies signatures; it cannot create them.  ~vellumory signatures
FILE~ prints the same verification as JSON.

** Validating PDF/A, PDF/UA and WTPDF
#+cindex: validation
#+cindex: veraPDF
#+cindex: PDF/A
#+cindex: PDF/UA
#+cindex: WTPDF
#+cindex: accessibility
#+findex: vellumory-validation-mode

PDF/A is the ISO standard for archiving, PDF/UA the one for accessibility,
and WTPDF (Well-Tagged PDF) its counterpart from the PDF Association.
veraPDF is the reference validator for them.  Vellumory runs it for you.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: V (vellumory-validate) :: Validate the document against a profile you
  choose, and show the report in =*Validation: NAME*=.

The profiles are: "auto" (whatever the document claims), PDF/A-1a, 1b,
2a, 2b, 2u, 3a, 3b, 3u, 4, 4e and 4f, PDF/UA-1 and PDF/UA-2, and WTPDF 1.0
for reuse and for accessibility.

The report lists the failed rules by specification and clause, each with
its failed checks.  The same check failing hundreds of times on a page is
one line with a count.  A check that names a page takes you there with
~RET~, and ~M-g n~ (~next-error~) walks through the checks from anywhere.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: n (vellumory-validation-next-rule) :: Next failed rule.
- Key: p (vellumory-validation-previous-rule) :: Previous failed rule.
- Key: b (vellumory-validation-beyond) :: Go to the checks beyond veraPDF
  (next section).
- Key: TAB :: Next button; ~<backtab>~, the previous one.

The verdict is veraPDF's.  PDF/UA has requirements that only a person can
judge, such as whether alternative text is meaningful or the reading order
makes sense, and the report says so.  Validation needs veraPDF, a Java
program, and a document read from a local file.

** Checks beyond veraPDF
#+cindex: preflight

Some problems matter whatever a document claims to be.  The validation
report shows them below veraPDF's results, and they appear at once,
because they take milliseconds.  On the command line, ~vellumory preflight
FILE~ prints them as JSON.

Errors:
- a damaged cross-reference table (readers repair it differently);
- links whose destination is missing or outside the document (MuPDF would
  silently send them to page 1);
- contents entries that lead nowhere;
- 40-bit encryption.

Warnings:
- RC4 encryption, which can be broken;
- earlier versions of the content still inside the file after incremental
  updates, and recoverable by anyone (not reported for signed documents,
  where incremental updates are expected);
- JavaScript;
- an XFA form;
- fonts that are not embedded;
- crop, trim, bleed or art boxes larger than the page.

Notes:
- composite fonts without a Unicode map, whose text cannot be copied or
  searched reliably;
- no title, no document language, no tagging;
- larger than 10 MB and not linearized;
- pages of different sizes.

Where Vellumory can fix a problem, the message names the key: ~e c~
rewrites the file (repairing it and removing earlier versions), ~e E~
encrypts with AES-256, ~e i~ sets the title and language, ~e L~ linearizes.

For the curious and the experts: Vellumory can show the document's raw

* Editing safely
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: Every edit recorded: pages, metadata, history, snapshots.
:END:
#+cindex: editing
#+cindex: safety

You can change documents in Vellumory without fear.  This is the promise:
no edit can lose an earlier version, and any state the document has been
in can be brought back exactly.

** How every edit is kept
#+cindex: snapshots
#+cindex: content-addressed store
#+cindex: atomic replacement

Every edit goes the same way:

1. It works on an unchangeable snapshot of the document, taken from a
   store in which each version is filed under the SHA-256 hash of its
   bytes.
2. The result is checked: it must open, and it must have pages.
3. The result is stored, and the edit is recorded in the document's
   history, with the time and a description.
4. Only then is your file replaced, in one step (a new file is written
   beside it and renamed over it), keeping its permissions.

Nothing in the store is ever deleted, and identical versions are stored
once.  Before restoring a snapshot, Vellumory checks it against its hash.
If the file was changed by another program since Vellumory last saw it,
that version is recorded first, as "changed outside Vellumory", so it is
never lost either.  Edits are deterministic: the same edit of the same
bytes gives the same result.  Two Emacs sessions, or Emacs and a script,
can edit the same document; their edits are taken one at a time.

After an edit the message names the new state, for example
=rotate pages 1 by +90° (14 pages) · state 1=, and how to undo it.

#+cindex: PDF/A, keeping
Edits keep archival documents archival.  Every rewrite puts the end of
line before each stream's end that PDF/A demands, and documents claiming
PDF/A-1 never get object streams, which PDF/A-1 forbids.  The test suite
checks with veraPDF that rotating, keeping pages, page labels, compression
and metadata edits leave PDF/A-1b and PDF/A-2b documents compliant.

All edit keys start with ~e~, and ~e~ in the command menu shows them grouped.

** Page edits
#+cindex: rotating pages
#+cindex: deleting pages
#+cindex: reordering pages
#+cindex: page ranges

- Key: e R (vellumory-rotate-pages-counterclockwise) :: Rotate

- Key: e d (vellumory-delete-pages) :: Delete the current page, or with a

** Document edits
#+cindex: metadata, editing
#+cindex: title
#+cindex: document language
#+cindex: attachments
#+cindex: associated files
#+cindex: stamps
#+cindex: watermarks
#+cindex: OCR
#+cindex: text recognition
#+findex: vellumory-metadata-mode
#+findex: vellumory-metadata-commit
#+findex: vellumory-metadata-cancel

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: e i (vellumory-edit-metadata) :: Edit the title, author, subject,
  keywords, creator, producer, language, whether viewers show the title
  instead of the file name, and the creation date, in a form buffer

In the metadata form, ~C-c C-c~ (~vellumory-metadata-commit~) records the
changes as one edit, and ~C-c C-k~ (~vellumory-metadata-cancel~) discards

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: e A (vellumory-attach-file) :: Embed a file in the document, under a

attachment becomes an associated file, with the relationship Unspecified

removed attachment leaves nothing behind in the file.

- Key: e o (vellumory-ocr) :: Recognize text with ocrmypdf, adding an
  invisible, searchable text layer.  You give the Tesseract languages

  text: =skip= them, =redo= an earlier OCR layer, or =force= recognition by
  rasterizing every page first.  This can take a while.
- Key: e f :: Fill in the form (see [[*Filling in forms]]).

** Compression and encryption
#+cindex: compression
#+cindex: linearization
#+cindex: encryption

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: e c (vellumory-compress) :: Compress losslessly: object streams, and
  every zlib-compressed stream recompressed at the strongest level.  The

  removes earlier versions left by incremental updates.
- Key: e L (vellumory-linearize) :: Linearize the document ("fast web view"),

** Undo and redo
#+cindex: undo
#+cindex: redo

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: C-/ (vellumory-undo) ::
- Key: C-x u ::
- Key: e u :: Go back to the state before the last edit.  Emacs's undo keys

- Key: e U :: Return to the most recent state after the current one.

Undo and redo rewrite the file from the stored snapshots, byte for byte.

** The history tree
#+cindex: history tree
#+cindex: branches
#+cindex: checkout
#+findex: vellumory-history-mode

When you undo and then make a different edit, the old line of edits is not
thrown away; the history branches, like a version control system.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: e h (vellumory-history) :: Show every state of the document as a tree,
  in =*History: NAME*=.

It looks like this (here after rotating page 1, undoing that, and then
keeping only the first three pages):

#+begin_example
~/papers/paper.pdf

○ 2026-10-29 22:01:15  opened (14 pages)  3662ff519e
├─ ○ 2026-10-29 22:01:15  rotate pages 1 by +90° (14 pages)  a428dc9047
└─ ● 2026-10-29 22:01:15  keep pages 1,2,3 (3 pages)  1548acec89

c make current  v view without changing the file  d compare with now  g refresh  q quit
#+end_example

The filled circle is the current state.  Each line shows when the state
was recorded, what the edit was, and the beginning of the snapshot's hash.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: RET (vellumory-history-checkout) ::
- Key: c :: Make the state on this line current.  The file is rewritten from
  its snapshot, and the reader shows it.  Nothing is lost: the state you
  left stays in the tree.
- Key: v (vellumory-history-view) :: Open the state on this line in a reader
  of its own, read-only, without changing the file.  Its buffer is named
  after the file and the state, as in =paper.pdf@1=.
- Key: d (vellumory-history-diff) :: Compare the state on this line with the
  current file (see [[*Comparing versions]]).
- Key: n (vellumory-history-next) ::
- Key: j :: Next state.
- Key: p (vellumory-history-previous) ::
- Key: k :: Previous state.
- Key: g (vellumory-history-refresh) :: Read the history again.

** Where the history lives
#+cindex: store location
#+cindex: VELLUMORY_STORE

The snapshots and histories are kept in =$XDG_DATA_HOME/vellumory=, which is
usually =~/.local/share/vellumory=.  This is durable data, not a cache:
=objects/= holds the snapshots, =history/= one small JSON file per document,
and =locks/= the files that keep two editors from interfering.  The
environment variable =VELLUMORY_STORE= moves the whole store elsewhere, which
is useful for experiments.

Two things are good to know:

- A document's history belongs to its path.  If you move or rename the
  file, its next edit starts a new history for the new path.  The old
  snapshots stay in the store, but the new history does not link to them.
- The store only grows: every state is a complete copy of the document,
  stored once.  There is no command to prune it yet.  Its files are ordinary
  files; deleting them is possible, but it takes away the ability to go
  back.

** Documents that cannot be edited
#+cindex: read-only files

Some documents are not edited, and Vellumory tells you why:

- A read-only file, or a file in a read-only directory: edits replace the
  file, so they need write access.  Write a copy and edit that (see
  below).  Reading, searching, inspecting and exporting work as usual.
- A converted document (DVI, PostScript, DjVu, office): the edit would not
  reach the original.
- A virtual document: edit its sources.
- A document that is not a local file (remote, or in a buffer of its own).
- A snapshot opened from the history tree with ~v~.

#+cindex: copy of a document

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: C-x C-w (vellumory-write-copy) :: Write a copy of the document to a
  file you name, make the copy writable, and visit it, so that you can
  edit the copy.  The suggested name is the document's with =-copy= added.
  In a reader, ~C-x C-w~ runs this command instead of ~write-file~.

The copy is of the PDF that Vellumory reads, so a converted, virtual or
remote document becomes an ordinary PDF file of its own, which can be
edited.

A signed document can be edited, but Vellumory asks first, because the edit
invalidates its signatures (see [[*Signatures]]).

* Comparing versions
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: A semantic comparison of two versions of a document.
:END:
#+cindex: comparing versions
#+cindex: diff
#+findex: vellumory-diff-mode

When a document comes back from a colleague, or after a series of edits,
you want to know what changed.  Vellumory compares what the document says,
not its bytes.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: d (vellumory-diff) :: Compare an earlier version (a file you choose)
  with this document, and show the result in =*Diff: NAME*=.

From the history tree, ~d~ compares any recorded state with the current file
(see [[*The history tree]]).

How the comparison works:

- Pages are paired by their text, so an inserted or deleted page does not
  make every later page look different.
- Paired pages are compared word by word.  Added words are highlighted in
  the reader (the face ~vellumory-highlight-added~) for as long as the
  comparison buffer exists; removed words are listed.
- Paired pages are also compared by their rotation and their media, crop,
  bleed, trim and art boxes, so a turned page shows up as, for example,
  "rotation 0° → 90°", even when its words are unchanged.
- Metadata, attachments and structure are compared field by field.

In the comparison buffer:

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: n (vellumory-diff-next) :: Next changed page.
- Key: p (vellumory-diff-previous) :: Previous changed page.
- Key: TAB :: Next button.

~vellumory diff OLD NEW~ prints the same comparison as JSON.

* Org mode and LaTeX
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: Links, accessible PDF export, SyncTeX, org-noter.
:END:
#+cindex: Org mode
#+cindex: LaTeX

Vellumory fits into the way many Emacs users write: notes in Org, papers
in LaTeX.  Links from your notes open the exact place in a document, Org
exports to PDF that is accessible and archival at once, LaTeX sources and
their PDF point to each other, and org-noter sessions run on Vellumory.

** Org links
#+cindex: Org links
#+cindex: vellumory links
#+cindex: org-store-link

Org learns a new link type, =vellumory:=, as soon as Org's link library
loads (after ~vellumory-install~).

To link to a place, run ~org-store-link~ in a document, then insert the link
in your notes with ~org-insert-link~ (~C-c C-l~).  The link records the exact
place, page and height:

#+begin_example
[[vellumory:~/papers/paper.pdf::7::312.5][“the trace tree”]]
#+end_example

That is page 7, 312.5 points from the top of the page.  The description is
the selected text, if you selected some; otherwise the nearest heading of
the document's contents with the page label; otherwise the file name and
page.

Following the link opens the document at that place, and
~vellumory-history-back~ returns to where you were.  When you insert a link
with ~C-c C-l~ and choose the =vellumory:= type, Vellumory asks for the
document and the page.

When Org exports a =vellumory:= link to HTML or Markdown, it becomes a link
to =FILE#page=N=, which browsers open at the page; LaTeX gets the same
through =\href=.  Other back-ends get the description alone.

#+cindex: org-pdftools
Notes written with org-pdftools use =pdf:= links, such as
=pdf:paper.pdf::7++0.42=.  Vellumory opens them, at the page and the height
fraction, provided no other package has defined =pdf:= links by the time
Vellumory's Org support loads (org-pdftools itself does, for pdf-tools).  Links to annotations or searches open their page.

** Exporting Org to accessible PDF
#+cindex: Org export
#+cindex: accessible PDF
#+cindex: LuaLaTeX
#+cindex: tagged PDF

Vellumory adds an Org export that produces PDF 2.0 that is accessible and
archival at the same time: PDF/UA-2, with a tagged structure (and MathML
for formulas), and PDF/A-4f.  It uses LuaLaTeX with LaTeX's new
=\DocumentMetadata= interface.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: C-c C-e l v :: In the Org export dispatcher, the entry "As
  accessible, archival PDF (Vellumory, validated)".
- Key: M-x vellumory-org-export-pdf :: The same as a command.  With a
  prefix argument, export only the subtree at point.

What happens:

1. Org writes LaTeX with a =\DocumentMetadata= line naming the document's
   language, the standards in ~vellumory-org-export-standards~ (by default
   =ua-2= and =a-4f=) and tagging, and LuaLaTeX compiles it.
2. The Org source is attached to the PDF (see [[*The Org source inside the
   PDF]]).
3. The PDF opens in Vellumory, and veraPDF validates it against each
   standard.  The echo area reports the results, for example
   =UA-2 ✓, A-4F ✓=; a standard that is missed opens its validation report.

The language comes from the file's =#+LANGUAGE:= line, and must be a
language tag such as =en-GB=; otherwise Vellumory asks you to fix it.  A
=#+LATEX_CLASS_PRE:= line in the file takes precedence over Vellumory's
=\DocumentMetadata= line, so you can set it yourself.  To target other
standards, change ~vellumory-org-export-standards~ (for example to
~("a-2b")~).

This needs LuaLaTeX from TeX Live 2025 or newer, with the LaTeX tagging
code (the packages latex-lab, tagpdf, pdfmanagement-testphase and luamml).
The project's flake pins such a TeX Live, and a test checks that veraPDF
accepts a real Org document (headings, a list, a table, a link, inline and
display mathematics) as PDF/UA-2, tagged, and PDF/A-4f.

** Exporting with Typst
#+cindex: Typst

If you prefer not to install TeX, Typst can do the same job.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: C-c C-e y v :: In the export dispatcher (with the ox-typst package),
  the entry "As PDF/A-3a and PDF/UA-1 (Vellumory, validated)".
- Key: M-x vellumory-org-export-typst :: The same as a command.

ox-typst writes Typst, and =typst compile --pdf-standard a-3a,ua-1= makes a
tagged PDF/A-3a that is also PDF/UA-1.  Typst refuses images without
alternative text, which is what accessibility asks for anyway.  The
language comes from =#+LANGUAGE:=; a region such as =en-GB= is passed the way
Typst wants it.  The result is validated against both standards and
opened, as with LaTeX.  ~vellumory-org-typst-standards~ chooses the
standards; Typst's PDF/UA-1 cannot be combined with PDF/A-4.  This needs
Typst 0.14 or newer (0.15.1 is the tested version) and the ox-typst
package.

** The Org source inside the PDF
#+cindex: embedded source
#+cindex: associated files

Both export routes attach the Org text to the PDF as its source file: an
associated file with the relationship Source and the type =text/x-org=.
Whoever has the PDF has what made it.  The text is the buffer as you
exported it, saved or not.

The attachment is an ordinary edit, so it appears in the PDF's history and
can be undone.  Standards that forbid it (PDF/A-1, PDF/A-2 and plain
PDF/A-4) get no attachment.  ~vellumory-org-export-embed-source~ turns this
off.

** SyncTeX and AUCTeX
#+cindex: SyncTeX
#+cindex: AUCTeX
#+cindex: forward search
#+cindex: backward search

SyncTeX connects a LaTeX source and its PDF: from a line of source to the
place it appears, and back.  Vellumory reads SyncTeX data with TeX Live's
libsynctex, the parser that Skim, Sumatra and Texworks use.  Compile with
SyncTeX on (in AUCTeX, turn on ~TeX-source-correlate-mode~), so that a
=.synctex.gz= file lies next to the PDF.

With AUCTeX, Vellumory becomes the PDF viewer when a TeX buffer starts
(~vellumory-sync-tex-viewer~).  It sets itself up after every other viewer
configuration, Doom's =:lang latex= included.

#+attr_texinfo: :compact t
- Key: C-c C-v :: In a TeX buffer, AUCTeX's ~TeX-view~: show the PDF with the
  line at point highlighted on its page.  This needs
  ~TeX-source-correlate-mode~; without it, the PDF simply opens.  Region
  compilation works too.
- Key: M-x vellumory-sync-forward :: The same without AUCTeX: show where the
  source line at point appears.  The PDF is the file of the same name with
  the extension =.pdf=.
- Key: C-mouse-1 (vellumory-sync-backward) :: In a document, visit the
  source line that produced the point you click.

The found lines are highlighted for ~vellumory-sync-flash-seconds~, and
~vellumory-sync-context-points~ of the page are kept visible above them.
If the source file has moved, Vellumory looks for a file of the same name
next to the PDF.

** org-noter
#+cindex: org-noter

org-noter keeps notes in step with a document.  Once org-noter is loaded,
its sessions run on Vellumory documents:

- the notes follow the page in view;
- a note opens its page, at its height;
- precise notes start at the selected text, or at a click on any visible
  page, not only the top one;
- the selected text starts the note's text;
- ~org-noter-create-skeleton~ turns the document's contents into headings.

Locations are stored in org-noter's own form, so notes made with other
org-noter back-ends keep working.  With evil, org-noter's ~i~, ~C-i~ and ~q~
take precedence over the reader's keys during a session.

* The command line
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: The vellumory program: inspect, validate and edit from scripts.
:END:
#+cindex: command line
#+cindex: scripting

The worker is also a command line tool.  Everything the reader does with
documents, scripts can do too, with the same results and the same
history.

#+begin_example
vellumory [SUBCOMMAND] [FLAGS]
#+end_example

=vellumory --help= lists the subcommands, and =vellumory SUBCOMMAND --help=
describes each one.  =--version= prints the worker's version.

Most subcommands print JSON on standard output.  When something goes
wrong, they print ={"error": "…"}= on standard error and exit with status 1.
The exit status tells you whether the command worked, not what it found:
a document that fails validation still exits with 0, and the =compliant=
field of the report gives the verdict.

** Inspecting from the command line
#+cindex: command line, inspecting

- ~vellumory info FILE~ :: The details report: format, structure, metadata,
  fonts, images and attachments, as JSON.
- ~vellumory text FILE~ :: The text of every page; pages are separated by
  form feeds.
- ~vellumory annotations FILE~ :: The annotations (not links or form fields)
  with their pages, as JSON.
- ~vellumory form FILE~ :: The form fields: name, type, value and choices.
- ~vellumory signatures FILE~ :: Verify every signature: integrity of the
  signed bytes, trust of the signer.
- ~vellumory preflight FILE~ :: The checks beyond veraPDF (see [[*Checks beyond
  veraPDF]]).
- ~vellumory validate [-f FLAVOUR] FILE~ :: Validate with veraPDF and print a
  normalized report.  The flavours are =0= (detect from the document's
  metadata, the default), =1a= =1b= =2a= =2b= =2u= =3a= =3b= =3u= =4= =4e= =4f=
  =ua1= =ua2= =wt1r= =wt1a=.
- ~vellumory batch-validate [-f FLAVOUR] FILE…~ :: Validate many documents in
  one veraPDF run and print one result per file.  With =0=, each file is
  checked against its own claim.
- ~vellumory check FILE~ :: qpdf's structural check, with qpdf's own output
  and exit codes (0 for clean, 2 for errors, 3 for warnings).
- ~vellumory qdf FILE~ :: Write the editable QDF rendering to the cache and
  print its path.
- ~vellumory diff OLD NEW~ :: The semantic comparison (see [[*Comparing
  versions]]): pages paired, changed words with their positions, structure.

For example, the beginning of a validation report:

#+begin_example
$ vellumory validate -f 1b paper.pdf
{"compliant":false,"profile":"PDF/A-1b validation profile",
 "statement":"PDF file is not compliant with Validation Profile requirements.",
 "passed_rules":123,"failed_rules":6, …}
#+end_example

** Editing from the command line
#+cindex: command line, editing

- ~vellumory edit FILE OPERATION~ :: Apply a recorded, reversible edit, given
  as JSON (next section).  The file is replaced atomically, and every state
  stays in the store.  Write =-= instead of the operation to read it from
  standard input; operations that carry passwords should always be passed
  that way.
- ~vellumory undo FILE~ :: Restore the state before the last edit.
- ~vellumory redo FILE~ :: Return to the most recent state after the current
  one.
- ~vellumory history FILE~ :: Print the branching history as JSON: every
  state with its index, parent, time, label, operation and hash.
- ~vellumory checkout FILE N~ :: Make state N current.  Nothing is discarded.
- ~vellumory snapshot FILE N~ :: Print the path of state N's snapshot in the
  store, without changing the file.

Each edit prints a short summary, such as:

#+begin_example
$ vellumory edit paper.pdf '{"op":"rotate","pages":[1],"degrees":90}'
{"changed":true,"head":1,"node":{"hash":"a428dc90…","parent":0,
 "operation":{"op":"rotate","pages":[1],"degrees":90},
 "label":"rotate pages 1 by +90° (14 pages)","index":1,…},"nodes":2}
#+end_example

The reader and the command line share the store and the history, so an
edit made in a script can be undone in Emacs, and the other way round.

** Edit operations
#+cindex: edit operations
#+cindex: JSON operations

An operation is a JSON object whose =op= field names it.  Pages are
numbered from 1.

| =op=                | Fields                                                                    |
|---------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =select=            | =pages=: the pages to keep, in order (reorders, deletes, duplicates)        |
| =rotate=            | =pages=, =degrees= (a multiple of 90; positive is clockwise)                |
| =append=            | =files=: documents whose pages are appended                               |
| =compress=          | none                                                                      |
| =linearize=         | none                                                                      |
| =labels=            | =specs=: qpdf page-label specifications, such as =["1:r", "5:D"]=          |
| =remove-labels=     | none                                                                      |
| =attach=            | =file=, =key=, optional =description= and =relationship=                     |
| =detach=            | =key=                                                                     |
| =metadata=          | =set= (an object), =remove= (a list), =date= (ISO 8601)                     |
| =ocr=               | =languages= (default =eng=), =mode= (=skip=, =redo= or =force=)                |
| =stamp=             | =file=, =under= (default false), =pages= (default =1-z=)                      |
| =encrypt=           | =user=, =owner=, =print= (=full=, =low=, =none=), =modify=, =extract=          |
| =decrypt=           | =password=                                                                |
| =fill-form=         | =values=: an object of field names and values                             |
| =annotate=          | =page=, =kind=, =quads= or =rect=, =color=, =contents=, =author=, =time=        |
| =annotation-edit=   | =page=, =object=, =contents=, =time=                                        |
| =annotation-delete= | =page=, =object=                                                          |
| =syntax=            | =qdf=: the path of an edited QDF file                                     |

The metadata fields are =title=, =author=, =subject=, =keywords=, =creator=,
=producer=, =language= (a language tag such as =en-GB=), =display-title=
(="true"= or ="false"=) and =created= (an ISO 8601 date).  =modify= is one of
=all=, =annotate=, =form=, =assembly= or =none=, and =extract= (true or false)
allows copying.  Annotation kinds are =Highlight=, =Underline=, =StrikeOut=,
=Squiggly= (on =quads=, eight numbers per marked line) and =Text= (at =rect=).

Some examples:

#+begin_example
vellumory edit report.pdf '{"op":"select","pages":[3,1,2]}'
vellumory edit report.pdf '{"op":"metadata","set":{"title":"Annual report","language":"en-GB"},"remove":["keywords"],"date":"2026-10-28T06:00:00+01:00"}'
vellumory edit scan.pdf '{"op":"ocr","languages":"deu+eng","mode":"skip"}'
echo '{"op":"encrypt","user":"","owner":"secret","print":"full","modify":"none","extract":true}' | vellumory edit report.pdf -
#+end_example

qpdf receives passwords through private files, never on its command line.

** Pages in and out
#+cindex: merging documents

- ~vellumory extract FILE PAGES OUTPUT~ :: Write the pages to a new document,
  keeping outlines and tags.  =PAGES= is a qpdf page range, such as =1-3,7=,
  =r1= (the last page) or =1-z:odd=.
- ~vellumory split FILE N PATTERN~ :: Split into documents of N pages; =%d= in
  the pattern becomes the page range, as in =out/part-%d.pdf=.
- ~vellumory merge OUTPUT FILE…~ :: Concatenate documents into a new one;
  document-level data comes from the first.
- ~vellumory compose~ :: Compose pages of several documents into one, as
  virtual documents do.  It reads a JSON list of parts from standard input,
  each with =file=, =first=, =last= (0 for the last page) and an optional
  =region= of four fractions (left, top, right, bottom), and prints the path
  of the cached result.

These leave their inputs unchanged.

** SyncTeX from the command line

- ~vellumory synctex [-c COLUMN] FILE SOURCE LINE~ :: Where a source line
  appears in the PDF (forward search).  =FILE= is the PDF, next to its
  =.synctex.gz=.
- ~vellumory synctexback FILE PAGE X Y~ :: Which source produced a point on a
  page (backward search), with =X= and =Y= in points from the left and top
  edges.

** For developers
- ~vellumory serve~ :: Serve the reader's protocol, JSON lines on standard
  input and output.  This is how Emacs talks to the worker; the protocol is
  internal and may change between versions.
- ~vellumory clip HEIGHT TOP BOTTOM~ :: Plan a clipped raster interval with
  the proved integer geometry; a small demonstration of the proofs at
  work.

** Sidebars and views under evil

In the contents sidebar: ~RET~ or ~go~ follows a heading, ~TAB~ folds and
~<backtab>~ cycles the whole outline, ~j~ and ~k~ move between headings, ~s~
switches to the previews, and ~q~ or ~o~ closes it.  In the page previews:
~RET~ visits a page, ~j~ and ~k~ move, ~s~ switches to the contents, and ~q~ or
~t~ closes them.  Marking pages for export works as in plain Emacs: ~m~ marks
the page at point, ~u~ unmarks it, ~U~ clears every mark and ~x~ exports the
marked pages.

#+kindex: zr (evil reading mode)
#+kindex: gO (evil reading mode)
#+kindex: g? (evil reading mode)
In reading mode the motions are evil's own, since it is text: ~j~, ~k~, ~C-f~,
~C-b~, ~gg~, ~G~ and the rest move through it as through any buffer.  On top of
them: ~q~ or ~zr~ returns to the pages, ~]]~ and ~[[~ go to the next and previous
page, ~gO~ to a heading of the contents, ~+~ and ~-~ change the text size and ~=~
resets it, ~C~ cycles the colours, ~TAB~ and ~<backtab>~ move between links, and
~?~ or ~g?~ opens reading mode's menu.

The keyboard caret's keys (see [[*With the keyboard caret]]) take precedence
over normal state while the caret is active.  In Vellumory's other buffers,
such as the history tree, the validation report, the comparison, the
details, the occur list, the syntax view, the batch table and the
annotation list, each buffer's own keys take precedence over normal
state.  So ~c~ in the history tree makes a state current rather than
starting evil's change operator.  In the syntax view, ~q~ closes the view
while it is read-only.

Doom's =:tools vellumory= module (see [[*Doom Emacs]] for installing it) sets
up:

- lazy loading: only the small file associations load at startup, and the
  reader loads with the first document;
- evil, when =:editor evil= is enabled;
- a doom-modeline segment with the page, the zoom, the search status and
  the caret, instead of the header line;
- a popup rule that shows page text on the right;
- document buffers as "real" buffers, so they join workspaces and appear in
  ~switch-to-buffer~ (the sidebars do not);
- ~ESC~ clearing search highlights first, then the selection;
- localleader keys with which-key descriptions, listed below;
- with =:lang latex=, Vellumory as AUCTeX's viewer, so ~SPC m v~ in a TeX
  buffer shows the line at point;
- in Org, ~SPC n l~ (~org-store-link~) stores the exact place in a document.

The localleader (~SPC m~ in Doom's default evil setup) in a document:

| Key | Command                        | Key   | Command                             |
|-----+--------------------------------+-------+-------------------------------------|
| ~o~   | contents                       | ~i~     | document details                    |
| ~t~   | page previews                  | ~v~     | validate                            |
| ~h~   | go to heading                  | ~d~     | PDF syntax                          |
| ~g~   | go to page                     | ~=~     | compare versions                    |
| ~l~   | go to page label               | ~c~     | cycle page colours                  |
| ~s~   | find text                      | ~w~     | fit width                           |
| ~S~   | clear search                   | ~p~     | fit page                            |
| ~f~   | follow link                    | ~2~     | two pages                           |
| ~y~   | copy selection                 | ~O~     | occur (all matches)                 |
| ~T~   | page text                      | ~r~     | reload                              |
| ~?~   | all commands                   | ~[~ ~]~   | back, forward                       |
| ~M~   | this manual                    | ~!~     | check the setup (doctor)            |

The localleader prefixes ~e~ (edit), ~a~ (annotate) and ~x~ (export) hold the
same commands as the plain Emacs keys: ~e r~ rotates, ~e u~ undoes, ~a h~
highlights, ~x x~ extracts pages, ~x s~ splits and ~x t~ exports the text.

In Dired, the localleader prefix ~v~ acts on the marked documents: ~v v~
validates, ~v e~ edits each, ~v i~ summarizes and ~v m~ merges.  The ~;~ keys of
plain Emacs work as well.

Every option can be set with Customize (~M-x customize-group RET vellumory~,

** Rendering

These options tune the renderer.  The defaults come from measurements (see
[[*Performance and how it is measured]]); change them only if you have a
reason.

- User Option: vellumory-strip-height :: Height in logical pixels of the
  image strips pages are made of.  Taller strips make scrolling cheaper,
  because redisplay costs about a millisecond per line of images in view.
  On the ISO 32000 standard, 192-pixel strips gave a mean scroll step of
  2.9 ms and a worst of 21–27 ms; 768-pixel strips 1.7 ms and under 5 ms,
  without rendering more pixels.  Default: ~768~.
- User Option: vellumory-tile-width :: Width of a tile when pages are wider
  than the window, so that only the visible columns are rendered.  Default:
  ~640~.
- User Option: vellumory-refine-delay :: Seconds without motion before
  previews are replaced by sharp tiles.  Default: ~0.09~.
- User Option: vellumory-immediate-budget :: Render sharp tiles at once, even
  while moving, if the worker's measured cost for this document says they
  take less than this many milliseconds.  Default: ~14~.
- User Option: vellumory-preview-scale :: Resolution of the whole-page
  previews shown while moving, relative to sharp tiles.  Default: ~0.25~.
- User Option: vellumory-prefetch-screens :: Screens rendered ahead of and
  behind the direction of motion when Emacs is idle.  Default: ~(1.0 . 0.5)~.
- User Option: vellumory-retain-screens :: Decoded images within this many
  screens of a window are kept; those further away are released.  Default:
  ~2.0~.
- User Option: vellumory-gc-threshold :: The garbage collection threshold
  while reading moves and while pages are laid out; garbage is collected
  once reading rests.  Default: ~67108864~ (64 MiB).
- User Option: vellumory-deep-rest-delay :: Seconds without reading after
  which the worker drops its caches: display lists, recent pages' text and
  decoded images, which make the next render fast and are made again when
  needed.  A scanned document of 10 pages kept 142 MB in the worker, 58 MB
  without them.  ~nil~ keeps them while documents are open.  Default: ~30~.
- User Option: vellumory-gc-idle-delay :: Seconds reading must rest before
  the garbage it left is collected, so that collections do not fall into a
  scroll step.  ~nil~ lets Emacs collect as usual.  When ~gcmh-mode~ is on (as
  in Doom), Vellumory leaves collection to it.  Default: ~0.5~.

** What Vellumory changes in Emacs
#+cindex: advice
#+cindex: remapped commands

So that nothing surprises you, here is every place where Vellumory
changes how Emacs itself behaves:

- ~vellumory-install~ adds a file name handler that visits documents
  without reading their bytes into the buffer (only for whole-file visits
  of files that open in Vellumory; ~vellumory-visit-without-reading~), and
  advises ~abort-if-file-too-large~ to skip the large-file question for
  documents.
- ~recenter~ is advised: in a window showing a document it keeps the place
  instead of scrolling.  Doom's window splits and evil's ~zz~ call it.
- Reader buffers turn off ~bidi-display-reordering~, which they do not need,
  and ~auto-hscroll-mode~, because Vellumory scrolls sideways itself.
- While the view moves, ~gc-cons-threshold~ is raised to
  ~vellumory-gc-threshold~, and garbage is collected once reading rests (see
  ~vellumory-gc-idle-delay~).  With ~gcmh-mode~ on, as in Doom, collection is
  left to it.
- ~save-place-mode~ is off for documents, and their old save-place entries
  are dropped.
- In documents, these commands are remapped: ~write-file~ to
  ~vellumory-write-copy~, ~undo~ and ~undo-only~ to ~vellumory-undo~, ~undo-redo~
  to ~vellumory-redo~, ~text-scale-adjust~ to ~vellumory-text-scale-adjust~,
  ~keyboard-quit~ to ~vellumory-search-quit~, and ~consult-line~ and Doom's
  ~+default/search-buffer~ to ~vellumory-consult-line~.
- Turning on ~follow-mode~ in a document turns on ~vellumory-follow-mode~
  instead.
- A function on ~TeX-mode-hook~ makes Vellumory AUCTeX's PDF viewer after
  every other setup; ~vellumory-sync-tex-viewer~ set to ~nil~ keeps yours.
- A function on ~enable-theme-functions~ redraws documents whose page
  colours follow the Emacs theme.
- With evil, evil-snipe is turned off in documents and their sidebars.

* Performance and how it is measured
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: What makes it fast, the method, the results and their limits.
:END:
#+cindex: performance
#+cindex: benchmarks
#+cindex: speed

Speed claims are only worth something with the method behind them.  This
chapter gives both, and says where the evidence ends.

** What makes it fast

- Rendering happens in the worker, on several threads, while Emacs stays
  free for your input.  Only strips that a window shows are rendered sharp,
  at the screen's resolution.  Work that scrolls out of view is cancelled.
- The worker writes raw pixels, which Emacs decodes fastest, to a directory
  in memory; nothing is compressed to PNG on the way.
- While the view moves faster than rendering, previews fill in; cheap
  pages render sharp at once; idle time prepares what comes next.
- Images rendered ahead are decoded before they scroll into view, not in
  the step that shows them.
- Reader buffers turn off Emacs's bidirectional text reordering, which
  they do not need: that cut the redisplay time of a scroll step from
  1.27 ms to 0.52 ms.
- Garbage collection is held back while you scroll and runs once reading
  rests, so it does not fall into a step.
- Opening reads page sizes from the document's page tree without parsing
  page contents, and hashes the file on another thread while MuPDF parses
  it.

** The method
#+cindex: measurement method

Two benchmarks are part of the repository and run from the development
shell.

The first, =tests/bench.el=, runs in a real graphical Emacs and drives each
reader through the same script on the same document and window.
"Blocking" is the time Emacs's main thread spends in a command plus a
forced redisplay: the delay a keypress experiences.  "Sharp" is the time
until every visible part of the window shows a final rendering.  Each run
uses a freshly started Emacs with byte-compiled code.

#+begin_example
lean --run scripts/Build.lean lake env lean --run scripts/Review.lean \
  bench target/corpus/pdf32000-2008.pdf
#+end_example

The second, the screen benchmark (=Review.lean screenbench=, with
=tests/screen-bench.el=), measures what reaches the display.  It records the
page area at 200 frames a second with ffmpeg while each viewer follows the
same script, and measures from the start of each action to the first
changed frame, and to the last change before the next action.  This
includes viewers that draw outside Emacs, such as EAF.

All figures below come from one machine: an AMD Ryzen 5 3600 (12
threads), with Emacs on a virtual X display (Xvfb).  They are ranges over
consecutive runs.

** The limits of this evidence
#+cindex: limitations of the measurements

- Every figure comes from one server on a virtual display.  No laptop or
  physical screen has been measured, and HiDPI has been checked only at
  GTK's scale 2 on a virtual display.
- The pgtk build of Emacs running on X11 added about 8 ms to every Emacs
  viewer's step: on the pinned Emacs 32 pgtk, forced onto X11, Vellumory's
  median step was 11.8–13.7 ms and its worst 19.9 ms, and the order of the
  viewers held.  Under Wayland, pgtk's own display, no screen recording was
  made.
- The numbers are for these documents and these scripts.  Other documents,
  with heavier pages, behave differently.
- Interpreted Lisp roughly doubles the layout and redisplay work, so the
  measurements are only meaningful with byte-compiled code.

The full record, with every run and the reasoning behind each design
choice, is in the repository: =docs/reader-validation.org= and
=docs/research.org=.

* Troubleshooting
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: When something does not work as expected.
:END:
#+cindex: troubleshooting
#+cindex: problems

Most problems have a simple cause.  Start with ~M-x vellumory-doctor~: it
checks the whole setup, names what is wrong and, where Emacs can fix it,
offers a button (see [[*Checking that it works]]).  Here are the problems
you are most likely to meet.

** The document opens in another mode
#+cindex: wrong mode

- ~vellumory-install~ has not run.  Call it in your init file (see [[*Setting up
  your init file]]), or open the file with ~M-x vellumory-open~.
- Another reader was installed after it.  pdf-tools' ~pdf-tools-install~, for
  example, puts its own association in front.  Call ~vellumory-install~
  afterwards.
- Your package manager does not load the generated autoloads.  Add
  ~(require 'vellumory-autoloads)~ before ~(vellumory-install)~.  In a plain
  clone, generate the file first (see [[*Setting up your init file]]).

** Emacs is too old
Vellumory needs Emacs 29.1 or newer, and the doctor says so.  Exporting
Org to accessible PDF also needs Org 9.7 or newer; the export names the
version it found (see [[*Requirements]]).

** Emacs says it needs a graphical display
The message reads "Vellumory needs a graphical Emacs with image support".
Pages are images, so start Emacs on a graphical display; ~emacs -nw~ and
text terminals cannot show them.

** The header line says the worker stopped
#+cindex: worker stopped
#+cindex: diagnostics

The header line reads "Worker stopped:" followed by the reason: the worker
program has ended.  Its messages are in the hidden buffer
=" *Vellumory worker diagnostics*"= (the name starts with a space; type it
with ~C-x b~ and a leading space).  Press ~r~ to reload: that starts a new
worker.  If the worker cannot start at all, check ~vellumory-program~: in a
shell, =vellumory --help= should print the subcommands.

** An edit says the file is read-only
Edits replace the file, so they need write access to it and to its
directory.  ~C-x C-w~ (~vellumory-write-copy~) writes a writable copy and
opens it; edit that (see [[*Documents that cannot be edited]]).

** Other edit refusals
- "Only documents visited from a local file can be edited": a remote file
  or a buffer without a file.  ~C-x C-w~ writes a local copy.
- "…is shown converted to PDF": converted documents cannot be edited.
- "A virtual document is composed from others": edit the sources.
- "A document needs at least one page": you tried to delete every page.
- "PDF/A-1 forbids the transparency highlights need": underline instead
  (~a u~).
- A question about a digital signature: see [[*Signatures]].

** Search finds nothing
The document may have no text layer: scans are pictures of text.  Add one
with ~e o~ (which needs ocrmypdf and Tesseract with your language's data).
If the text is there but search still misses it, try a regular expression
search (~C-M-s~); some documents encode characters oddly, and the preflight
checks (~V~, then ~b~) report fonts without Unicode maps.

** Validation, OCR or export complain about a missing program
Each of these runs a separate program: veraPDF for validation, ocrmypdf
for text recognition, LuaLaTeX or Typst for Org export, a converter for
DVI and the others.  Install the one named in the message (see
[[*Requirements]]).  The Nix build of the worker includes qpdf, veraPDF and
ocrmypdf; other builds look for them on your =PATH=.  In Doom, =doom doctor=
lists what is missing.

For the LaTeX export, TeX Live must be 2025 or newer and include the
tagging code; the message "#+LANGUAGE must be a language tag" means that
the file's =#+LANGUAGE:= line should read like =en-GB=.

** Pages look blurry for a moment
That is the preview shown while you move faster than pages can be
rendered.  It sharpens about a tenth of a second after you stop
(~vellumory-refine-delay~).  If pages stay blurry, the worker may be busy
with a heavy page; the header line says "Rendering…" until it is done.

** SyncTeX finds nothing
Compile with SyncTeX on, so that =NAME.synctex.gz= lies next to =NAME.pdf=.
In AUCTeX, turn on ~TeX-source-correlate-mode~ before compiling.  Forward
search names the source line it looked for when it finds no match.

** vellumory-info cannot find the manual
Info has to find =vellumory.info=.  See [[*Reading this manual in Emacs]].

** Evil keys do not work
Call ~vellumory-evil-setup~ after evil has loaded (see [[*Setting up evil]]).
In Doom, enable =:editor evil= and the module does it.

** Where Vellumory keeps its files
#+cindex: disk usage
#+cindex: files Vellumory writes

| What                                           | Where (by default)                                  |
|------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| reading positions                              | =~/.local/state/vellumory=                          |
| snapshots and edit histories                   | =~/.local/share/vellumory=                          |
| previews kept across sessions (256 MB at most) | =~/.cache/vellumory/rasters=                        |
| page sizes of documents opened (same budget)   | =~/.cache/vellumory/rasters/sizes=                  |
| QDF files, virtual documents, attachments      | =~/.cache/vellumory=                                |
| copies of remote documents, conversions        | =~/.cache/vellumory=                                |
| the session's rendered strips                  | under =$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/vellumory=, removed on exit |

Caches can be deleted at any time.  The store in =~/.local/share/vellumory=
is not a cache: it holds your edit histories.

* Frequently asked questions
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: Short answers to common questions.
:END:
#+cindex: questions
#+cindex: FAQ

** Does reading a document change the file?
No.  Reading never writes to the document.  Your place is saved elsewhere
(see [[*Leaving and coming back]]), and saving the buffer writes nothing.
The file changes only when you make an edit, and every edit can be
undone.

** Can I use Vellumory alongside pdf-tools?
Yes.  Whichever was installed last opens PDFs by default, and ~M-x
vellumory-open~ always opens a file in Vellumory.  Vellumory reads pdf-tools'
=.vpdf= virtual documents, and opens org-pdftools' =pdf:= links when nothing
else claims them.

** How much disk space does the history take?
Every state is a complete copy of the document, stored once however often
it recurs.  A 2 MB paper edited ten times takes up to 22 MB in the store.
There is no command to prune the store yet.

** Can I edit the text on a page?
No.  Vellumory edits a document's structure, not its page content.  For
the raw PDF, the syntax view (~C-c C-c~) lets an expert change objects
directly.

** Does it work on macOS or Windows?
Nobody has tried yet.  Vellumory is developed and tested on Linux.  The Nix
flake also declares Apple silicon Macs (=aarch64-darwin=), and CI builds it on
macOS 26 and runs its tests there on every change: the Lean suites, the proof
audit, the Emacs tests, and the graphical tests in Emacs's Cocoa build (all
32 pass).  No person has used it on a Mac yet.
Intel Macs are out, since nixpkgs 26.11 dropped
them.  Reports from other systems are welcome.

** Can I print?
Not from Vellumory: there is no print command yet.  Print the file with
your system's tools.

** Can I sign a document?
No.  Vellumory verifies signatures, but cannot create them.

** Why is the worker written in Lean?
Lean 4 is a programming language and a proof assistant.  The worker is
ordinary, fast compiled code, and some of its parts come with proofs: that
the render queue hands out the most urgent job and loses none when work is
cancelled, that the edit history stays well-formed, that clipped pixels
stay inside the page and the window, that arguments reach external
programs unchanged, and that the comparison's alignment is sound.  The
project's tooling (build, tests, reviews, benchmarks) is Lean too.

** Why does my EPUB have different page numbers after changing the text size?
A book has no fixed pages; Vellumory lays it out on pages of a given size.
Larger text needs more pages.  Vellumory keeps you at the same share of
the book.

* Credits
:PROPERTIES:
:DESCRIPTION: The projects Vellumory builds on and learned from.
:END:
#+cindex: credits
#+cindex: acknowledgements

Vellumory stands on the work of many people.

It renders and reads documents with MuPDF, by Artifex.  Its edits run on
qpdf, by Jay Berkenbilt, whose fix-qdf also makes the syntax view
editable.  Validation is done by veraPDF.  Text recognition is ocrmypdf with Tesseract.  Signatures
are verified with OpenSSL, XMP metadata is edited with Exempi and Adobe's
XMP Toolkit, and SyncTeX data is read with TeX Live's libsynctex.  The
worker is written in Lean 4, with Lake and the lean4-cli library.  Org
export uses LuaLaTeX with the LaTeX Project's tagging code, and Typst.
And all of it lives in GNU Emacs, with Org and transient.

The test documents include =tracemonkey.pdf= and =alphatrans.pdf= from
Mozilla's pdf.js test suite, and Adobe's published copy of ISO 32000-1.

Vellumory learned from the Emacs document readers before it, and is
grateful to their authors: DocView, which comes with Emacs; pdf-tools,
whose features and file formats set the bar, and whose users' wishes
shaped this work; emacs-reader, which showed what a MuPDF module can do;
EAF's PDF viewer; doc-tools; and org-noter, whose notes Vellumory is glad
to serve.  Its evil keys follow evil-collection's for pdf-tools, and its
presentation and caret browsing follow Evince, Okular and Firefox, and
its link previews GNOME's Papers.  Reading mode lays out its text with
Emacs's own HTML renderer, shr.  Fitting
the content width follows pdf-tools' slicing and Sioyek's smart fit.  The
rendering design learned from PDF.js, Chromium's PDFium viewer, Okular,
Evince, zathura and Sioyek.  The Doom module builds on Doom Emacs and
nix-doom-emacs-unstraightened.
