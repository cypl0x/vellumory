#+title: Reader validation
#+date: 2026-10-28

* Rendering pipeline (protocol 2)
Emacs sends JSON lines to a per-buffer Lean worker. Each open copies the
document's bytes into a MuPDF document (PDF, XPS, EPUB, CBZ, FB2, images),
hashed with OpenSSL SHA-256 on a separate thread while MuPDF parses.

- Page boxes come from page-tree attributes (=pdf_page_obj_transform=), no
  content or annotation parsing; =loadedPageSizes= cross-checks them in tests.
- Rendering runs on N dedicated threads (default min(6, cores − 2)), each
  borrowing a cloned MuPDF context. Document access holds =doc_lock=; display
  lists (LRU of 24) are replayed concurrently. Threads waiting for the same
  page reuse the first thread's list instead of rebuilding it.
- Each request has a MuPDF cookie: =cancel= removes queued work and aborts
  running renders; aborted lists are never cached.
- Strips and tiles are written as raw binary PPM to a per-worker tmpfs
  directory (=$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR=), LRU-bounded (128 MiB since 2026-10-30, 512 before) and removed on exit.
  No PNG encoding anywhere on the hot path.
- Highlights (search, selection, link hints) and page recolouring (theme tint,
  luminance inversion) are composited by MuPDF into the raster; the raster key
  includes them, so clearing returns to cached plain tiles with zero new pixels.
- The queue is a proved priority queue (=Vellumory/Scheduler.lean=): pop
  returns a minimal element and a permutation of the rest; cancellation keeps
  no cancelled id and loses no job.

Emacs side:
- Pages are rows of tiles (one tile per row at fit width; 640 px tiles beyond
  the window width). Only tiles intersecting a window are rendered sharp, at
  device resolution (=frame-scale-factor=), displayed at logical size.
- Up to threads + 1 requests in flight, ordered from a reading focus line at
  30% of the window height; obsolete requests are cancelled immediately.
- Adaptive refocus: while moving, tiles whose estimated raster cost exceeds
  14 ms get a whole-page preview (sliced into every strip) and refocus after
  0.09 s of rest. Cost is learned from pure raster time per megapixel, kept
  separate from list-building time (a preview needs the same display list,
  so it never helps with interpretation cost). Jumps render sharp at once.
- Idle prefetch: one viewport ahead and half behind in the direction of motion.
- Decoded images are kept within 2 screens of a window and released beyond.
- Visiting a document reads no bytes into the buffer (a narrowly scoped
  =insert-file-contents= handler); saving can never write the layout.

Read with care:
- Open is not the same act: the desktop readers start a process (window,
  fonts, session bus); Vellumory opens in a running Emacs, as before.
- No GPU here: GTK 4 finds no EGL display on Xvfb and draws Papers with its
  cairo renderer (=GSK_DEBUG=renderer=: GskCairoRenderer), on the CPU. Papers'
  CPU and its first frame are not what it does with GL or Vulkan.
- Papers animates a Down step for 200 ms, Okular every step for 100 ms (its
  smooth scrolling, on by default): their settled times are the animation.
- The X server's CPU over the steps was 0.9–1.15 s for Vellumory on X11,
  0.7–1.2 s Evince, 1.1–1.8 s Okular, 1.3–1.5 s Papers, of which about 1.1 s is
  the 200 fps capture alone (1.08 s over 27 s with nothing drawn); pgtk forced
  onto X11: 1.9–2.2 s.
- Vellumory's RSS leaves out its strips on tmpfs, 38 MiB (tracemonkey) and 42
  MiB (ISO 32000) at the end. Split at the end, ISO 32000: Emacs 97 → 175 MB
  during the run, worker 118 → 141 MB; tracemonkey: Emacs 70–94 → 136–142 MB,
  worker 44–54 → 79–86 MB. A plain =emacs -Q= frame: 70 MB.
- One Down step in four of five runs (two of three on ISO 32000) took 16–30 ms
  in Vellumory; two further runs listed step by step (=--steps=) had none over
  7 ms. Not attributed yet: these runs log no garbage collections.
- Wheel notches 0.6 s apart, because closer ones reach Emacs as double and
  triple clicks (=double-click-time= 0.5 s) and =vellumory-wheel= scrolls 80
  pixels times the click count: ten notches 40 ms apart, as a spun wheel
  sends them, scrolled 4400 pixels, twenty 16800 (0.6 s apart: 800); GTK
  scrolls =page_size^(2/3)= and Okular 100 pixels a notch, whatever the pace.

On this machine Vellumory answers a step within the first frame, as Evince does,
and jumps as fast as the fastest; Okular's and Papers' steps take 4 to 10 times
longer to show, by animation (and here, for Papers, software composition). It
uses more memory than any of them: about 70 MiB more than Evince on the short
paper (110 with its tmpfs strips), 140 MiB more on ISO 32000 (180 with them).

** Prefetched tiles are decoded ahead (2026-10-28)
Emacs decodes an image when it is first drawn (=lookup_image=), about 5 ms for
a 768 px strip. A tile rendered ahead of the view is now decoded when its
raster arrives (=image-size=), between input events, instead of in the scroll
step that shows it; ISO 32000 reading-scroll worst steps stayed at 11 ms while
opening measured 90–96 ms.

** One worker for every document (measured, 2026-10-28)
The worker used to be one process per document: a worker with one small
document has 40.5 MB resident and 10 threads (6 render, 1 document, runtime),
so five documents cost at least 200 MB and 50 threads, five render pools
competing without a common priority. One worker now serves every document of
the session (requests name their document, =close= frees one, the last buffer
stops it): five documents (three fixtures, the slides, tracemonkey.pdf) open in
one process of 47.5 MB and 10 threads, its render pool shared by priority and
one raster budget.

** Bidirectional reordering (measured, 2026-10-28)
A reader buffer holds only images and spaces, yet Emacs reordered every
redisplayed line for bidirectional text: interleaved measurements of one
scroll step's redisplay gave 1.27 ms with =bidi-display-reordering=, 0.52 ms
without. Reader and presentation buffers now turn it off (on again only when a
page label has right-to-left characters, for the header and mode line); the
reading-scroll median fell from 1.3–2.1 ms to 0.4–0.8 ms, below pdf-tools'
1.0–1.6 ms.

** Strip height (measured, 2026-10-28)
Redisplay costs about 1 ms per line of images in view, so the default strip
height went from 192 to 768 logical pixels (=vellumory-strip-height=). Same
benchmark, fresh byte-compiled daemons: 192 px, reading mean 2.9–3.7 ms and
fast-scroll worst 21–27 ms; 384 px, 2.1–2.4 and 3–12 ms; 768 px, 1.7–1.8 and
2.8–4.4 ms. Rendered megapixels did not grow (192: 7.5–9.5, 384: 6.3–7.8,
768: 5.9–9.3): idle prefetch already renders around the view. These numbers
are for one machine and document; no claim beyond them is made.

** Garbage collection waits for reading to rest (measured, 2026-10-29)
On the screen benchmark, one or two of thirty reading steps took 17–20 ms
instead of 2–7 ms, in some runs only. Logging =gcs-done= with each action
placed every slow step on a collection; a collection costs about 15 ms in that
Emacs (14.6 ms measured; doc-tools' 13.2 ms), and lands in a step's frame
whenever it falls between the step's Lisp and its redisplay. Vellumory
allocated 93 KB per step cycle (14 KB in the step itself), so with Emacs's
default 800 KB threshold it collected every ninth or tenth step. Moving the
view (=vellumory--seek=, so scrolls, jumps, animation frames and follow mode
alike, and any command in reader, sidebar or presentation buffers) now raises
=gc-cons-threshold= to =vellumory-gc-threshold=, and collects once reading has
rested =vellumory-gc-idle-delay= (0.5 s) with no frames being drawn. Idle
timers proved the wrong clock: Lisp run while Emacs is already idle (the
benchmark's emacsclient script, a reload, follow mode) fired them inside the
step, making things worse (a collection every fifth step, jumps 22 ms). Left
to =gcmh-mode= when on (Doom: 64 MB, idle collection) and to the incremental
collector. Eight runs after: no collection during the thirty steps, worst step
6.6–8.8 ms; 60 steps at ten a second, none; 1.2 s after, the threshold back
at 800 000 and one collection.

** New windows keep the place (fixed, 2026-10-29)
Emacs gives a window split off another, or a window showing a buffer again,
the old window start but not its vscroll; Vellumory's strips are 768 pixels,
so such a window came up to a strip above the place. A window made shorter (a
split below, a popup) lost it too: point stayed on a row now below the window,
and redisplay scrolled to it, a whole strip on (1352 → 2120 pixels). Now a
window newly showing a document shows it where it last did, or where the
latest window on it is (as image-mode does for DocView and pdf-tools), or
where reading last was; a resized window is placed again at its own pixel;
a relayout keeps every window's place, not only the first's. Found by the
isolated Doom suite, whose fit-content check sat on a page's white margin
after a split had moved it to the top. Two more, found by keys in Doom on :1:
=delete-other-windows= recomputes the remaining window's start and resets its
vscroll (Emacs 31 window.c, "Reset the vscroll, as redisplay will not"), so
every move now records the window's place with its size and a resize alone
puts it back; and Doom's split commands call =recenter= afterwards, which in
a reader only scrolled the page (point is an anchor on a visible strip), so
=recenter= keeps the place in reader windows. After both, splitting below and
beside, closing the others, a help popup and zz leave page 11 at y 158.6.

** Where the time went (fixed, measured before/after)
- PNG encoding: 4–5× worker render cost → raw PPM.
- Worker startup: =import Lean= made =main= call =lean_initialize()= for the
  whole compiler (Lean/Compiler/LCNF/EmitC.lean): 40 ms and 75 MB RSS →
  dependency-free =Vellumory.Json=: under 10 ms and 17 MB.
- SHA-256: MuPDF ~0.3 GB/s → OpenSSL (SHA-NI) 1.96 GB/s, overlapped with
  parsing: worker open 130 → 36 ms for 22 MB.
- Page labels: =fz_load_page= per page under =doc_lock= (175 ms for 756
  pages) → =pdf_page_label= (navigation reply 59 ms after open).
- Redundant display-list builds by threads queued on =doc_lock= (4× one page).
- Emacs: triple layout on open, DocView MD5 when opened via another mode,
  reading the 22 MB file into the buffer, sidebar visibility recomputed every
  refresh, GC bursts (3 collections, 93 ms, during one jump), and per-pixel
  recomputation of raster work while scrolling within the same rows.

* Raster comparison: an explicit limitation
Text and axis-aligned rectangle fixtures match whole-page rendering byte for
byte across strips and tiles. The diagonal fixture differs slightly at band
edges (max 13–14/255 in at most 1% of channels), reproduced exactly by
=mutool draw= with and without banding: MuPDF's band rasterization, not ours.
